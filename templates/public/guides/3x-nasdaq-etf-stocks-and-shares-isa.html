The short answer

Yes, usually. UK investors who want three times the daily move of the Nasdaq-100 inside a Stocks & Shares ISA typically use a London-listed, Irish-domiciled exchange-traded product rather than a US fund. A common example is the WisdomTree NASDAQ 100 3x Daily Leveraged product, ticker LQQ3 on the London Stock Exchange (ISIN IE00BLRPRL42).

Whether you can buy it depends on your platform. Check the exact ticker in your own ISA before you plan around it.

Why not the US funds?

Well-known US leveraged funds are generally not offered to UK retail investors. UK platforms usually need a UK-compliant key information document for each fund they sell, and many US funds don't provide one. Rules here have been changing, so check what your own platform offers rather than relying on an old forum thread.

How a 3x daily product works

  • The leverage resets every day. If the Nasdaq-100 rises 1% today, the product aims to rise about 3% before costs. Tomorrow it starts again from the new level.
  • Over longer periods it is not "three times the index". Because of that daily reset, a month or a year can end well above or below three times the index return. Our guide to volatility decay works through the numbers.
  • It has running costs. There is an ongoing charge, and the leverage itself has a cost. Check the issuer's factsheet for current figures.
  • It is quoted in pence. LQQ3 trades in GBX, so a price of 25,000 means £250. See GBX vs GBP.

The risks, plainly

A 3% daily fall in the index is roughly a 9% fall in the product. Deep index falls can wipe out most of its value, and it can take years to recover, if it does. Product terms also allow the issuer to close or restructure a product in extreme conditions. A leveraged product is not a long-term buy-and-forget holding for most people.

Many investors who use one hold it as only part of a portfolio, next to something much steadier such as cash or a short-dated bond fund, and rebalance on a fixed schedule. One example is quarterly signal rebalancing.

What the ISA changes

Inside a Stocks & Shares ISA, gains and income are free of UK income tax and capital gains tax, and you don't report them on a tax return. You can pay in up to the annual ISA allowance across all your ISAs (see GOV.UK: Individual Savings Accounts). Selling and buying inside the ISA doesn't use up allowance; only new money paid in does.

Checklist before you buy

  1. Find the exact ticker and ISIN in your own platform's ISA, not just in a search engine.
  2. Read the issuer's key information document and factsheet.
  3. Note whether the line is quoted in GBX or GBP.
  4. Decide in advance what share of your portfolio it may be, and when you'll rebalance.
  5. Write the plan down, or use a tool that keeps you to it.
