Why spreadsheets are popular

A spreadsheet is free, flexible and yours. For a quarterly signal plan you need only a few columns: date, deposits, holdings, prices and the target. Plenty of people run one for years.

The six usual mistakes

  1. Stale prices. A live price function silently returns yesterday's or last week's close, or nothing at all, and the sheet carries on.
  2. Pence treated as pounds. A GBX price is used as GBP and the holding looks 100× bigger. See GBX vs GBP.
  3. Missed quarter ends. The plan only works if you act shortly after each quarter. A forgotten check-in means trading on old numbers.
  4. Mid-quarter deposits. Money added during the quarter has to be dated correctly, or the target drifts.
  5. Trades that differ from the plan. You meant to buy 7.2 shares and bought 7. If the sheet records the plan rather than the fill, it slowly diverges from reality.
  6. Overwritten history. Editing last quarter's row "to fix it" breaks every number after it.

A checklist for any tracker

  • Every price has a date next to it, and you know its unit.
  • Every deposit, withdrawal, buy, sell, dividend and fee is recorded with date, quantity and price.
  • Corrections are new rows, not edits to old ones.
  • You have a reminder for each quarter end.
  • Holdings and cash match your broker after each trade.

What an app can add

A dedicated tracker can check prices on a schedule, refuse to give an instruction when a price is stale or in the wrong unit, remind you at quarter end and keep an append-only history of what you actually did.
